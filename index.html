01 — Real-time multiplayer Node · Express · Socket.IO · Postgres

Six phones,
one bomb,
one server
telling the truth.

A live party spelling game. One person hosts a room; everyone else joins from their own phone or laptop with a link or a four-letter code. The bomb says a word, you type it before the fuse burns out, and the bomb moves one seat clockwise whether you spelled it or blew it.

What it is

Rooms of up to eight, hearts from one to five, four difficulty tiers with a growth curve, sixteen word categories over a 6,196-word bank, a seeded daily challenge, and three bots to fill a quiet room. Two turn styles: Passed Bomb, where the bomb walks the seat order, and Everyone Spells, where the whole room races the same word.

What was hard

Keeping six devices on six networks agreeing about one fuse. The server owns rounds, hearts, the timer, word selection, grading, placements and every reward; clients send intents and render what they are told. A disconnected player's seat, hearts and streak survive a reconnect, and a heartbeat of state resync means a phone that slept mid-round rejoins the round already in progress rather than a stale copy of it.

Two decisions worth defending

No dark patterns. No timers, chest slots, expiry, forced openings, paid currency, fake scarcity or hidden odds. The odds table the shop renders is literally the same object the server rolls against, and the whole economy lives in one file so nothing else can invent a number.

The README corrects itself. An earlier version claimed the word was sent only to whoever's turn it was. That was not true — speech synthesis needs the text, so every listening client has it. The mode-by-mode consequences are now written down, including the one where it is exploitable.

02 — Local-first health tracking TypeScript · React · PWA

A month of
your own data,
and no server
to hand it to.

You build a daily check-in around your own situation — skin, gut, migraine, POTS, fatigue, a diet experiment — answer it in about a minute, and watch the trends come out over weeks. There is no account and no server, because there is nothing to upload it to.

Longitudinal, not diagnostic

Twelve question packs — eczema, carnivore, POTS, IBS, migraine, MCAS, fatigue, autoimmune, thyroid, joint pain, general wellness, wearable — can be mixed, switched off individually, reordered and extended with your own questions, each with independent visibility across five surfaces. Possible-pattern cards need at least six paired days before they will say anything, and they never claim a cause.

Not medical advice. The app surfaces possible patterns in your own logs and says so on the card itself.

The one exception, drawn to scale

  • written notes
  • photos
  • your name
  • anything identifying

  • the labels of metrics you track

Optional AI observations are off by default, use a key you bring, and a test asserts each of those exclusions. A locally stored key is not encrypted — Settings says so, rather than implying a vault.

03 — Computer vision Python · OpenCV · SQLite · static site

A broadcast
goes in.
Evidence
comes out.

An esports broadcast is a video of a game, not a record of one. This turns a full OWCS VOD into an auditable hero-composition timeline — and every fact it publishes can be clicked back to the exact frame it was read from.

Knowing when you are wrong

Matching a 35×35 pixel portrait against a template set is the easy half. The detector returns a ranked candidate, its runner-up, and the margin between them — and returns UNKNOWN rather than a guess when that margin is thin. HUD geometry is found computationally (HSV chip rows, a RANSAC grid fit, pixel-evidence verification) and the calibrator refuses below a confidence floor, with reasons, instead of producing a layout nobody should trust.

Swaps are temporal, not per-frame

Replays, killcams and scoreboards look exactly like gameplay to a template matcher, so a structural filter classifies every frame before it is allowed to create a composition. Hero swaps come from a consensus model with hysteresis — persistence plus displacement — which is why the Nepal map produced two confirmed swaps with before/after evidence crops and rejected seven lookalikes.

Where a model may speak

Two jobs here are language problems in computer-vision clothing: an OCR'd nameplate reading TW1STED M1NDS, and a calibration refusal like "b3 portrait box has almost no detail (texture 31)". An optional advisor handles those two and nothing else — closed vocabulary, only after the deterministic path fails, every return marked binding: false, and an assertion that raises if one ever reaches a persistence path.

And here is what it cannot do.

Quoted from the project's own README, which keeps this list near the top rather than the bottom.

  • Hero coverage is 8 of 52 on the best template package. The other 44 have never appeared in a broadcast this pipeline has processed, so no amount of reprocessing covers them.
  • juno and kiriko are confusable at 35×35 in grayscale. A colour veto was built, measured against real crops, and removed because it blocked none of the actual leaks.
  • One map, one match. Thresholds are fitted to a single broadcast, and the map winner is operator-supplied rather than OCR'd.

04 — Generative audio React 19 · Web Audio · PWA · MIT

Every sound
in this app is
made at runtime.

Write the words you want to hear, choose a voice, and let them loop over ambient sound for as long as you like. The repository ships no audio samples at all — every ambience is synthesised in the browser — so the download stays small and it works offline forever with no cache to miss.

Audio claims that are measured, not asserted

The test suite runs against a real Web Audio implementation in Node. It builds the same graph a browser builds, renders it, and measures the result: every brainwave preset's modulation rate must land within 0.05 Hz of its target over a 30-second render, and each binaural channel's pitch is measured separately — which only agrees with the intended pair if the channels really are hard-split.

Transitions are tested by interrupting a render partway through and calling the production API at the context's own time, then asserting that the largest sample-to-sample jump never exceeds the tone's own slew rate. No click, proven rather than hoped for.

An hour of audio without 635 MB of RAM

Rendering a full session in one offline context would need roughly 635 MB. Instead the export renders in chunks through OfflineAudioContext — many times faster than real time, in a Web Worker so the app stays usable — and stitches them into a real MP3 you can put on any player.

The speech uses the device's own engine through the Web Speech API: no cloud service, no key, no cost. Every available voice is scored and labelled honestly — Neural, Enhanced, Standard, or Basic — robotic, worth avoiding — and when the best one on the device is poor, the app names the exact settings path to download a free neural voice.

05 · Tiny Vials three.js · 600+ tests

Seventeen real interactions,
graded in real units.

A 3D phlebotomy practice game where nothing is a multiple-choice question. The angle you entered at. The seconds the tourniquet was on. The millilitres in each tube. The grams of pressure you held, and for how long. Screw the needle into its holder and the bevel ends up pointing wherever your turning stopped — the next step inherits exactly that.

The body under your hands can go wrong, caused by what you did, and it announces itself the way it would at the chair. Stopping the draw is often the right answer, and it really does stop the draw: the report is built from the tubes you actually collected.

06 · Remy Dee: The Lost Lexicon one HTML file · 1.08 MB

You learn medical terms
by building them.

A player who finishes it can look at a term they have never seen, break it into prefix / root / suffix, and make a confident, correct guess at what it means — without having felt like they were studying. Four beats, one vocabulary, four cognitive modes: construction, recall, automaticity, transfer. The design doc's rule is that the systems must never let the player skip a mode.

The idea, in one term

brady-slow + -cardiaheart condition = bradycardiaa slow heart rate

Two parts you already know, one term you have never seen.

Every mechanical advantage traces back to a word the player understood. The stated anti-goal is blunt: if a player can win by ignoring the vocabulary, the game has failed. The whole thing is a single HTML document with no build step, and the effects layer feature-detects each library so it stays playable if all of them fail.

07 · Phlebotomy Exam Prep TypeScript · static export

Know what to study
before the exam.

A certification study platform built around one loop: learn → practise → get feedback → find the weak area → drill it → test. The results screen leads with the weak-area insight, not the score. Mastery per area is weighted toward recent answers, gated on exposure, and shows its reasoning in plain language.

The Order of Draw drill offers three interchangeable interactions — drag, tap-to-swap, and move up/down buttons on every row — so the knowledge being tested is never gated behind a pointer gesture.

08 · TwoDo TypeScript · Postgres RLS

Privacy proven in SQL,
not in a component.

The action layer for couples: discover something that works for both of you, agree privately, decide together, turn it into a real plan, and learn from what happened. Distances are shown as times from a traffic-aware corridor model, because twenty miles is eighteen minutes or an hour depending on the clock.

Neither partner sees the other's vote until both have responded — enforced by row-level security in Postgres. CI runs an authorization suite against a real PostGIS container that proves, in SQL, that one partner cannot read the other's private gift ideas, cannot see a claim on their own wishlist, and cannot read a vote before casting their own.

Two refusals worth copying:

TwoDo never says a venue is allergy safe — the strongest claim it will make is possible fit, confirm with the venue. A booking is only “confirmed” when a provider confirms it; otherwise the state is deep-link-opened, and it says so.

08 — Method

The connecting
theme is not
a stack.

A multiplayer game, a health journal, a vision pipeline and an audio engine look scattered until you read the code, at which point the same five decisions keep reappearing. They are the actual portfolio.

  1. i

    Say UNKNOWN rather than something plausible.

    A detector that returns its runner-up and its margin. A calibrator that refuses below a confidence floor and prints why. A study platform that leaves the official exam structure undefined. Being wrong quietly is the only failure mode that compounds.

  2. ii

    One authority per fact.

    SpellBomb's server owns every outcome and the client may only ask; its whole economy — odds, prices, pity, duplicates — lives in one file that the shop renders from and the server rolls against. A number with two sources is a bug waiting for a release.

  3. iii

    The honest limits go at the top of the README.

    “Hero coverage is 8 of 52.” “All 94 questions sit at needs-review.” “Everyone Spells is exploitable and here is why.” Written by the person who would most like them to be untrue.

  4. iv

    Correctness lives in pure functions.

    Wheel selection, couple-fit scoring, mastery calculation, rubric grading, chest odds, breathing timings — all pure, all tested without a DOM, a clock, or a network. The UI is allowed to be messy; the thing that must be right is not.

  5. v

    Motion serves the content or it gets deleted.

    The WebGL ambience layer in the tracker was removed because it cost more than the rest of the page combined and made targets move away from the pointer. Manifester declined Vanta on payload grounds. Reduced motion is honoured from the operating system, not just an in-app toggle.

Real-time systems

Authoritative server state over Socket.IO, reconnection and seat recovery, per-IP connection caps, graceful shutdown, Postgres with a file fallback, load testing.

Vision & pipelines

OpenCV template matching, RANSAC geometry fitting, temporal consensus models, staged SQLite ingestion, evidence archives, reproducibility gates.

Local-first products

IndexedDB, PWAs, offline-first sync-free architectures, persistent-storage negotiation, versioned schema migrations that never throw.

Real-time media

Learning systems

Mastery estimation, weak-area detection, deterministic recommendation, rubric design, question-bank integrity testing.

Craft

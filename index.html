001

001 — Computer vision Python · OpenCV · SQLite

A broadcast
goes in.
Evidence
comes out.

An esports broadcast is a video of a game, not a record of one. OWCSComp.Tracker turns a full OWCS VOD into an auditable hero-composition timeline — and every fact it publishes can be clicked back to the exact frame it was read from.

The interesting part isn't the detection.

Matching a 35×35 pixel portrait against a template set is the easy half. The hard half is knowing when you are wrong. The detector returns a ranked candidate, its runner-up, and the margin between them — and returns UNKNOWN rather than a guess when that margin is thin. HUD geometry is found computationally (HSV chip rows, a RANSAC grid fit, pixel-evidence verification) and the calibrator refuses below a confidence floor, with reasons, instead of producing a layout nobody should trust.

Replays, killcams and scoreboards look exactly like gameplay to a template matcher, so a structural filter classifies every frame before it is allowed to create a composition. Hero swaps are not read from single frames but from a temporal consensus model with hysteresis — persistence plus displacement — which is why the Nepal map produced two confirmed swaps with before/after evidence crops and rejected seven lookalikes.

Where a language model is allowed to speak.

Two jobs in this pipeline are language problems wearing computer-vision clothing: an OCR'd nameplate reading TW1STED M1NDS, and a calibration refusal like "b3 portrait box has almost no detail (texture 31)" — precise, correct, useless to a human. An optional advisor handles those two and nothing else.

Its suggestions are closed-vocabulary (it may only return an id from a list it was handed), it runs only when the deterministic path has already failed, every return value is marked binding: false, and an assertion raises if one ever reaches a persistence path. It contributes nothing to any published number. Seventy-four offline checks exercise it against a fake provider; most of them test the refusals.

And here is what it cannot do.

Quoted from the project's own README, which keeps this list at the top rather than the bottom.

  • Hero coverage is 8 of 52 on the best template package — the other 44 have never appeared in a broadcast this pipeline has processed, so no amount of reprocessing covers them.
  • juno and kiriko are confusable at 35×35 in grayscale. A colour veto was built, measured against real crops, and removed because it blocked none of the actual leaks.
  • Thresholds are fitted to one broadcast. Re-measure when a second is harvested.
  • One map, one match. Series-level scores are not recorded; the map winner is operator-supplied, not OCR'd.

002

002 — Simulation JavaScript · three.js · 600+ tests

Seventeen
real
interactions.

A 3D phlebotomy practice game where nothing is a multiple-choice question. You run a small draw room, and everything the game grades is something you physically did — measured in real units.

The angle you entered at. The seconds the tourniquet was on. The millilitres in each tube. The grams of pressure you held, and for how long. Screw the needle into its holder and the bevel ends up pointing wherever your turning stopped — the next step inherits exactly that. Choose a butterfly set on the back of the hand and it is a genuinely different procedure: 23G, a different entry window, tubing that transmits every tug to the tip.

The body under your hands can go wrong, caused by what you did, and it announces itself the way it would at the chair — a lump rising, flow stopping, a face going damp and quiet. You get one answer. Stopping the draw is often the right one, and it really does stop the draw: the report is built from the tubes you actually collected. Then the laboratory looks at what you sent, and a rejection names the tests the patient has to be drawn again for.

Clicking through cannot out-earn technique. That is arithmetic, not policy — the reward function reads the same 0–100 measurement the rubric grades from, so payout and assessment can never disagree.

Palpation, as code

  • A vein bounces.
  • A tendon is a hard cord.
  • An artery pulses.
  • A compliant vein rolls away from the pressure — you can feel it move.

Eight ways it fails

  • hematoma
  • blown vein

  • nerve contact

Mode 01

Learn

Full teaching. Names the specific error, states the correct next action, and will not let you continue until the step is right.

Mode 02

Practice

A standing reminder of what each step is for, and nothing about what is currently wrong with it. Measurements arrive at the end of a section.

Mode 03

Final Practical

Silence, then a rubric report: 0–4 per row, automatic-failure events, and a replay built from the steps' own logs.

Bests are tracked per mode, because 26/28 with the coach talking is not the same achievement as one scored in silence.

003

003 — Local-first TypeScript · React · PWA

Zero
network
requests.

A daily health journal that lives on your device. You build a check-in around your own situation — skin, gut, migraine, POTS, fatigue, a diet experiment — answer it in about a minute, and watch the trends come out over weeks. When you have an appointment, you print a summary and take it with you.

Out of the box, after the app has loaded once, it makes no network requests at all. There is no account and no server, because there is nothing to upload it to. Twelve question packs can be mixed, switched off individually, reordered, and extended with your own questions — each with independent visibility across five surfaces.

The flip side of local-only is stated plainly rather than glossed: nobody can recover it for you. So the app nudges you toward a real backup file, explains that installed apps are exempt from browsers' idle-eviction rules, and asks for persistent storage on your behalf — then shows you whether the browser actually granted it.

The one exception, drawn to scale

Never leaves the device

  • written notes
  • photos
  • your name
  • anything identifying
  • excluded questions
  • entries outside the window

Sent, only after you confirm a preview

  • the labels of metrics you track
  • one row per logged day of numeric answers, days numbered from the start of the window rather than dated

Optional AI observations are off by default, use a key you bring, and a test asserts each of those exclusions. A locally stored key is not encrypted — Settings says so, rather than implying a vault.

“No model ID is load-bearing.” The first version hard-coded a model name. Google retired it for new keys months ahead of the published date, and every new user got a 404 from a build that had worked the week before. 004

004 — Generative audio React 19 · Web Audio · PWA · MIT

This app
contains no
audio files.

A tiny calm garden for your intentions: write the words you want to hear, choose a voice, and let them loop over ambient sound. Every sound in it is synthesised in the browser at runtime — so the download stays small, it works offline forever with no cache to miss, and the repository ships no samples at all.

Audio claims that are measured, not asserted

The test suite runs against a real Web Audio implementation in Node. It builds the same graph a browser builds, renders it, and measures the result: every brainwave preset's modulation rate must land within 0.05 Hz of its target over a 30-second render, and each binaural channel's pitch is measured separately — which only agrees with the intended pair if the channels really are hard-split.

Transitions are tested by interrupting a render partway through and calling the production API at the context's own time, then asserting that the largest sample-to-sample jump never exceeds the tone's own slew rate. No click, proven rather than hoped for.

Exporting an hour of audio without 635 MB of RAM

Rendering a full session in one offline context would need roughly 635 MB. Instead the export renders in chunks through OfflineAudioContext — many times faster than real time — and stitches them into a real MP3 you can put on any player.

The speech itself uses the device's own engine through the Web Speech API: no cloud service, no key, no cost. Every available voice is scored for quality and labelled honestly — Neural, Enhanced, Standard, or Basic — robotic, worth avoiding — and when the best one on the device is poor, the app names the exact settings path to download a free neural voice.

005 — Learning game One HTML file · 1.08 MB · 69 commits

You learn
medical terms
by building
them.

Remy Dee: The Lost Lexicon. A player who finishes it can look at a term they have never seen, break it into prefix / root / suffix, and make a confident, correct guess at what it means — without having felt like they were studying.

Knowledge is power, literally

Every mechanical advantage traces back to a word the player understood. Momentum comes from chaining real terms; good materials come from a correct decode; gate answers open the fast lane. There is no stat you can buy that replaces knowing a root. The stated anti-goal is blunt: if a player can win by ignoring the vocabulary, the game has failed.

The whole game is one file

No build step, no bundler, no install — a single HTML document you can open. GSAP, three.js, Vanta and Lenis are loaded from a CDN and the effects layer feature-detects each one, so the game is fully playable if any or all of them fail. The font stylesheet was moved off the critical path after a measurement on a network that couldn't reach the CDN: the title screen used to take 12.9 seconds to appear. And the operating system's reduced-motion setting is authoritative unless the player explicitly opts back in.

006 · Phlebotomy Exam Prep

Know what to study
before the exam.

A certification study platform built around one loop: learn → practise → get feedback → find the weak area → drill it → test. The results screen leads with the weak-area insight, not the score. Mastery per area is weighted toward recent answers, gated on exposure, and shows its reasoning in plain language.

The Order of Draw drill offers three interchangeable interactions — drag, tap-to-swap, and move up/down buttons on every row — so the knowledge being tested is never gated behind a pointer gesture.

007 · TwoDo

Privacy proven
in SQL, not in a
component.

The action layer for couples: discover something that works for both of you, agree privately, decide together, turn it into a real plan, and learn from what happened. Distances are shown as times from a traffic-aware corridor model, because twenty miles is eighteen minutes or an hour depending on the clock.

Neither partner sees the other's vote until both have responded — enforced by row-level security in Postgres. CI runs an authorization suite against a real PostGIS container that proves, in SQL, that one partner cannot read the other's private gift ideas, cannot see a claim on their own wishlist, and cannot read a vote before casting their own.

Two refusals worth copying:

TwoDo never says a venue is allergy safe — the strongest claim it will make is possible fit, confirm with the venue, and an unverified allergy pushes a result down. A booking is only “confirmed” when a provider confirms it; otherwise the state is deep-link-opened, and it says so.

09 — Method

The connecting
theme is not
a stack.

Six domains in five weeks looks scattered until you read the code, at which point the same five decisions keep reappearing. They are the actual portfolio.

  1. i

    Say UNKNOWN rather than something plausible.

    A detector that returns its runner-up and its margin. A calibrator that refuses below a confidence floor and prints why. A study platform that leaves the official exam structure undefined. Being wrong quietly is the only failure mode that compounds.

  2. ii

    A claim is worth what its evidence chain is worth.

    Every published composition links comp → run → frames → crops → review status; if the chain is missing, the page shows nothing rather than a number. Every question carries a review status. Every audio claim is measured from a real render.

  3. iii

    The honest limits go at the top of the README.

    “Hero coverage is 8 of 52.” “All 94 questions sit at needs-review.” “The partner switcher is a demonstration device, not a security boundary.” Written by the person who would most like them to be untrue.

  4. iv

    Correctness lives in pure functions.

    Wheel selection, couple-fit scoring, mastery calculation, rubric grading, reward arithmetic — all pure, all tested without a DOM, a clock, or a network. The UI is allowed to be messy; the thing that must be right is not.

  5. v

    Motion serves the content or it gets deleted.

    The WebGL ambience layer in the tracker was removed because it cost more than the rest of the page combined and made targets move away from the pointer. Manifester declined Vanta on payload grounds. Reduced motion is honoured from the operating system, not just an in-app toggle.

Research

Alzheimer’s & dementia
education.

Health Science Research Methods, CSU Channel Islands. I was one member of a student team, and the description below is sized to what the study supports.

105

participants surveyed

The question. Does a short, structured educational intervention change how confident people feel about Alzheimer’s disease and dementia — recognising it, talking about it, and supporting somebody living with it?

The method. Confidence measured before and after, across a sample of 105 participants.

The finding. The post-intervention confidence measure improved.

What it does not show. A convenience sample with no control group shows a short intervention can move self-reported confidence. Not a population, and confidence is not capability.

This is the project that changed how I build software. Every product here now has a rule about what it may claim: Health Journal’s evidence ladder, PaperAnimator’s refusal to write a sentence the paper does not contain, OWCS Comp Tracker returning UNKNOWN rather than a guess. One habit, learned here.

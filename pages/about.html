How I work

Five rules I did not
set out to have.

They came out of the work — an ambulance, a research methods course, a club roster and about a hundred thousand lines of trying things.

01

State the finding at its correct size

Research Methods taught me to describe a result without inflating it, and that is now a design constraint: Health Journal shows no confidence percentages, because a percentage is a promise about a population and a journal is a sample of one. OWCS Comp Tracker returns UNKNOWN rather than a best guess. PaperAnimator cannot write a sentence the paper does not contain.

02

Design for the worst minute

Field medicine trains you for what happens when things are already going badly, which in software means degradation over demo. Health Journal works with the network off; Manifester falls back to a device voice rather than going silent; SpellBomb’s privacy page has no stylesheet, script or font, so it renders on a dead connection. This site is fully readable with JavaScript switched off.

03

Make the thing legible, then make it beautiful

Running a club is mostly reducing what people have to hold in their heads. Interfaces are the same question: what is the one number this screen is about, what does it cost to find it, and what can go without losing anything. Beauty comes after that, and then it matters enormously — a calm screen is the product working.

04

Write down the limits

Every repository here documents what is not production-ready, what is not proven, and where each number came from. OWCS Comp Tracker’s honest-limits section is the most useful page in it; SpellBomb’s README corrects a claim an earlier version of itself made. That is not humility — it is how you still trust your own system a year later.

05

Finish it

Anyone can start. The long tail is the work — the reconnect path, the empty state, the keyboard route through a drag, the export that has to open in real software, the test that fails on a content mistake before it reaches a student. Six products got that far. Check that claim first.

Taste

What I want a screen
to feel like.

Cinematic and immersive, which in practice means far fewer elements than people expect, treated far more carefully.

Typography

Bold display, quiet body

A display face for the two or three things that matter, a very readable one underneath it, and monospaced labels for anything technical. Three faces on this page, plus a mono.

Motion

Meaning, not decoration

Every project animates the way it behaves — SpellBomb burns a fuse, Health Journal grows, Manifester breathes, PaperAnimator unfolds. All of it stops dead under a reduced-motion preference.

Atmosphere

Near-black, warm ivory, one accent

A warm dark ground, a real paper surface for the page that is a document, and one colour per page, taken from the product it is about.

Sound

Only when asked

I build audio products and I do not autoplay audio. Manifester speaks when you press play; SpellBomb pronounces a word when a round starts. Nothing else on this site makes a noise.

Simplicity

Elegant, not empty

The difference is whether what was removed is still doing its job somewhere. Manifester keeps five things on the Create screen and the rest one row away — nothing was taken out, it was put in order.

Navigation

Never an experiment

Atmosphere is never worth an interaction somebody has to decode. The menu opens with JavaScript switched off, and every link is a real link.

Outside the work

Sunlight, the gym,
the beach, the game.

Most of this started as something I wanted to understand, not something I wanted to build.

Light, sleep and the everyday variables

Circadian rhythm, morning light, vitamin D, nutrition, training load: the unglamorous inputs with the strongest evidence and the least attention. Health Journal’s sun sessions exist because I wanted the real solar elevation curve for my own latitude and date, and an estimate that plateaus — a number that keeps climbing is quietly telling you a longer burn is a bigger benefit.

Human performance

The most legible feedback loop I have: a variable you control, a measurement, and a delay before the answer. The same shape as every experiment in Health Journal, which is not a coincidence.

Competition

Overwatch, Deadlock and Marvel Rivals, and running the club around them. A fast education in deciding on incomplete information, reading a system while it is still moving, and the fact that the best team is the one that communicates clearest, not the one with the best player.

Building things

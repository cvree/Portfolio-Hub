PaperAnimator

Read the paper. Mark it up. That is the presentation.

Open a research paper and get a source-faithful animated presentation you can edit, verify and export. Everything runs in the browser: the PDF is read on your own machine, nothing is uploaded, nothing is stored, and nothing is used for training.

01

What it is

An editor that opens on the real pages of a paper, rendered as printed, with text you can drag across the way you would in any PDF reader — and on an empty storyboard. Nothing is composed on your behalf, because a talk assembled by a machine is a talk you then have to argue with. (If you would rather begin from a draft, the empty storyboard offers to make one from the whole paper; it stays a thing you asked for.)

Highlight a passage and a bar arrives on it. Then either click an instrument or pick one up and drop it on the words:

  • Statement — the passage, set as large as it fits, read aloud as it appears.
  • Big number — the statistic at full size, with the sentence that qualifies it.
  • Pull quote — a quotation, attributed to its page.
  • Build a list — one line per sentence, each arriving as it is spoken, each separately cited.
  • Beat by beat — one scene per sentence: a paragraph becomes a sequence in one gesture.
  • Figure — a crop of the page at full resolution, with its caption.
  • Spotlight — those exact words marked inside the sentence around them.
  • Side by side — this passage against one you kept earlier, in two columns.

02

Why I built it

Health Science Research Methods taught me to read a paper properly and then asked me to present one. Those are two completely different skills, and the gap between them is where accuracy goes to die. You read carefully, you build slides, and somewhere in the retyping a hedge falls off a sentence, a confidence interval gets left behind, and a “was associated with” quietly becomes a “caused”.

Every tool that offered to help made it worse, because they all generate. A summariser produces fluent sentences that the paper does not contain, and then you are proofreading a machine instead of presenting a study.

I wanted the inverse: a tool that is structurally incapable of writing a sentence the paper does not say. That constraint is the product.

03

The most interesting interaction

The highlight is the citation. When you mark a passage, it carries the quads of what you marked — down to the character. That is not metadata attached afterwards; it is the scene’s source. So there is no later step where a citation gets added, and therefore no later step that can be skipped or got wrong.

The consequences run in both directions and both are visible on screen. Passages already used by a scene are washed in mint on the page, with a numbered chip in the margin that jumps to that scene — so you can see, on the paper itself, what you have already covered. And clicking a claim on the canvas draws a thread back to the exact words it came from.

While you carry an instrument, the scene it would make floats under the cursor — drawn by the same renderer as the canvas and the export, so the preview cannot promise what the drop would not deliver.

None of it needs the pointer. Click a line to take its sentence, ⌥↑ to widen the mark to its paragraph, then press a letter: S statement, N number, Q quote, B build, X beats, F figure, H spotlight, V voice-over. M opens the animation gallery for whatever you are looking at.

04

The problem being solved

Two guarantees, and both are enforced by the architecture rather than by discipline.

The composer selects; it never writes. Every on-screen atom and every narration cue is either a span of the source or a linking phrase that carries no claim. Comprehension is treated as a ranking problem over the paper’s own sentences, so the result is grounded by construction rather than grounded by review. Each atom carries exactly one provenance label: extracted, paraphrase, explanation, connective, authored or unsupported. A paper that never states its limitations gets an empty limitations list, and the interface says so — because that absence is information about the paper.

Playback is a pure function of time. One function, resolveFrame(project, tMs), is the only source of animated values, and it is imported by the live canvas, the static previews and the exporter alike. Nothing inside the scene surface animates via CSS or a motion library. That single rule turns “preview matches export” from a QA problem into a type signature — and a harness draws both renderings of the same frame side by side to prove it, while asserting the reduced-motion contract: with movement off, nothing travels.

The same discipline governs the animation catalogue. Twenty-one entrances and six sustained motions live in one file, each a pure function of progress. The storyboard’s animated tiles run that function, the stage runs it, and the exporter runs it — so an animation cannot look one way while you are choosing it and another way once it is chosen.

05

Current capabilities

  • A real reader. Page rasters as printed, a real selectable text layer, and a dock of instruments you drop onto what you marked.
  • Eight instruments, each producing a scene that already carries its source, plus Add to this scene and a voice-over cue.
  • An animation gallery where every tile is your own scene, animating for real — so choosing is watching rather than guessing. The gallery leads with the entrances built for whatever the scene is actually about, judged by what occupies the most of the frame.
  • Three further controls: sustained motion after the entrance has landed, choreography that hands out delays so elements arrive in sequence, and a volume control that takes the same move louder or quieter without changing its character. Every choice applies immediately and is a single undo away.
  • Project-wide Source Integrity, telling you how much is grounded and which parts need review before you present it.
  • Exports that open in real software. MP4 (H.264 through WebCodecs, with the timestamp each frame represents so the duration is the real one, falling back to WebM where H.264 cannot be written — and saying so), a ZIP of one PNG per scene, an editable PPTX whose speaker notes carry the narration and its page citations, a searchable PDF with an invisible text layer and a source appendix, SRT and VTT caption tracks, a timed transcript with the page each line came from, and a project file that reopens with its assets.
  • A sample paper generated in the browser and run through the same pipeline your own paper would take — so there is something real to try without uploading anything.
  • Twelve verification harnesses under tools/, each driving a real browser against the production build: the flow, the reader at phone and tablet widths, motion, export parity, every export run for real, playback sampled from the exported video, and a keyboard-only accessibility path.
06

Design & technical decisions

Comprehension is selection, not generation. The plan this was built from routed comprehension through a hosted model. The implementation ranks the paper’s own sentences in the browser instead. That is a stronger accuracy guarantee — a component that only ever chooses existing sentences cannot invent one — and it removes the account, the key and the network round trip in the same move.

There is one source view, not two. The plan offered an outline mode beside a pages mode. The outline is gone: a re-typeset paper is not the paper, and having two notions of “where you are” made the product feel like a tool rather than like reading.

Narration is honest about what it cannot do. Narration is read by the browser’s own speech engine, which cannot be captured into a video file. So the video is silent and carries captions, and the captions, transcript and speaker notes contain every spoken line. The documentation says this in the exports table rather than leaving somebody to discover it after a render.

Where the build departs from the plan, the build is the record. Ten design documents sit in the repository — data model, extraction pipeline, render engine, design system, editor spec, export spec, quality gates, roadmap — and the two places the implementation diverged are written up as departures with their reasoning, rather than quietly edited to match.

07

What changed across iterations

Plan

Ten design documents before a line of code — product definition, non-negotiables, architecture, risks, and a definition of done. Two of its assumptions did not survive contact.

Then

The outline mode was removed. A re-typeset paper is not the paper; the reader now shows real page rasters with a real text layer over them.

Then

The hosted model was removed. Ranking the paper’s own sentences in the browser is both more accurate and less to explain.

Then

One renderer for the canvas, the previews and the export. The determinism contract that made “what you see is what you get” provable rather than aspirational.

Then

The animation gallery became your own scenes. Generic preview tiles replaced with the actual scene, animating, so the choice is made by watching.

Latest

MP4 by default, with WebM as the fallback — H.264 through WebCodecs, and an export that tells you when it had to fall back rather than handing you a file that will not open.

08

What comes next

  • Narration in the video file. The single biggest gap. Browser speech cannot be captured, so it needs an on-device synthesiser rendering to a buffer the muxer can take — which is a problem I have now solved once already, in Manifester.
  • Tables as first-class as figures. The extraction pipeline reads them; there is no instrument that turns one into a scene yet.
  • Multi-paper comparison. Side by side currently pairs two passages from the same paper. Journal club wants two papers.
  • A verification pass with people who present research for a living. Source Integrity is my model of what grounding means, and it deserves to be tested against somebody else’s.

Where to find it

Press “Try a sample paper” on the landing page — it generates a real two-column PDF in the browser and runs it through exactly the pipeline your own paper would take. There is no server, no account and no API key, so nothing you open ever leaves your machine.

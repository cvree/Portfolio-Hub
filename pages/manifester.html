Manifester

Your own words, read back over a room that breathes.

Write the words you want to hear, choose a voice, and let them loop over ambient sound for as long as you like. It speaks in its own voice — the same one on every device — keeps every line it has spoken so a loop plays offline, and installs to a phone’s home screen.

01

What it is

Two halves. Create is the workspace: a title, a text box that takes one line or a twenty-minute script, a session length, and a live preview that breathes with your pattern, cycles your lines as the loop will read them, and takes its colour from the ambience. Player is the calmest screen in the app and now the whole of it — the breath, the words being spoken, the controls that matter, nothing else.

02

Why I built it

The unglamorous levers on how a day goes — sleep, sunlight, breathing, what you say to yourself before something hard. Breathing has the best evidence and the worst software: apps that want a subscription before they will say a sentence, that cannot work on a plane, and that read your words back in a robotic voice that undoes the point.

I wanted the version where the words are yours, the voice is good, it works with the network off, and none of it is a negotiation. It is also the hardest audio problem I have taken on: speech, layered ambience, a breathing clock and a session timer all staying in step while the user changes things underneath them.

03

The most interesting interaction

Changing the background sound without stopping the session. A control in the corner of the stage — one of three things on screen in the expanded view — opens one list: silence, every built-in ambience, every sound you have imported, and your playlist.

Pick one and it crossfades under the running loop. The voice, the clock and the breath keep going; the pass count does not reset; nothing restarts. The same from the Adjust sheet and from the Library.

That sounds small and is the hardest thing in the product: the moment a session stops to accommodate a preference, the session is over and you are operating software again.

The same rule governs the rest of it. Voice, speed and pitch change without leaving the screen: the running loop is re-optioned rather than restarted, so each change lands on the next line. The delay between loops counts down on screen — “next loop in 6s” — and freezes where it is if you pause.

04

The problem being solved

A browser’s built-in speech is not the same product on two devices. The same code sounds like a different app on a Pixel, an iPhone and a Windows laptop — and on many the default is a legacy formant synth that makes a calming line sound like a train announcement. A loop you saved has to read the same way wherever you play it, or you do not own it.

So it ships two studio voices of its own — Ivy and Fen, Kokoro-82M, on this project’s own service, free, with no account, key or metering. And it caches hard: a line heard once plays instantly forever after, offline, because every clip is keyed by a hash of exactly what it is.

The fallback is designed, not accidental. Device voices remain for anyone who prefers one, and they read the words if the studio voice cannot be reached — a session never goes quiet because a network did. They are scored by quality, the best wins automatically, and each is labelled honestly: Neural, Enhanced, Standard, or Basic — robotic, worth avoiding. When the best one is poor, the app gives the exact settings path to a free neural voice for that platform.

One written line is one spoken line. The voice takes a line at a time and reports back the moment it starts, so the player shows that text. Only a line too long for one breath is split further — sentence, then clause, then word — which also stops browsers cutting a long passage off part-way.

05

Current capabilities

  • A breathing guide in eight forms with ten patterns, five breath voices and custom timing for every phase, reporting phase progress on a thin ring with the phase name and a countdown in the centre. It pauses and resumes with the session.
  • Six rooms. The whole screen expands and settles with the guide: Atmosphere, Rings, Waterline, Curtains, Starfield, Stillness — or Drifting, which moves between all six, ninety seconds at a time, crossfading over four. Each is a circle centred on the orb, measured rather than guessed, and nothing with an edge crosses it.
  • An expanded view, one tap from the corner of the stage: the player fills the screen and the header and navigation step back until only the words, the breath and the controls remain. Same player throughout — session, clock, pass count and audio carry straight on.
  • Everything else in one place. Adjust opens a single sheet, in the order things are wanted, on phone and desktop alike. Choosing a row closes it and opens the one it is about, rather than stacking a second scrim on the first.
  • Download the loop as a real MP3 — five minutes to two hours, your voice, your delay, your background sound, your balance. Rendered on your device in a Web Worker with a WAV fallback, so the app stays usable and nothing is uploaded. From any saved loop, not just the one in front of you.
  • Nothing you play is lost. Starting a session writes it to the library on the way to the player, so words you listened to but never saved wait under Recent plays. The last twelve are held; nothing you saved is pruned.
  • An offline writing helper that reads what you wrote, works out what it is reaching for, and appends lines in the same direction — or rewrites it into the present tense and first person, turning an avoided feeling into the thing you want. One tap each, both undoable.
  • Optional AI, with your own key. One provider, one guided flow that spells out every step and its cost. Off by default, and it falls back to the offline engine whenever the network or the key gives out.
  • Backend-free sharing, an optional calendar event, and listening totals kept on the device and shown without streaks or goals.
06

Design & technical decisions

Returning is one step. The app restores the last played loop on launch, so the common path is open → play. Five things stay on the Create screen — title, words, length, Start, Save — and the rest lives behind Customize your ritual, rows that state their own value (Moon Garden · 40%, Calm · 4 in / 6 out, 3-second delay) and open in a sheet.

The mixing desk had to go. A column of settings beside the stage made a ritual space look like a control surface, and on a phone it put those controls below a full-height stage: clutter, and out of reach. Nothing was removed — same live sliders, same sheets — but there is one control now instead of two layouts.

A key is checked by using it, never by looking at it. Connect spends one short real request, and “connected” means it came back. Manifester used to insist a Google key began AIza; when AI Studio moved to keys beginning AQ., every new key in the world was refused before a request was made. Both work now, so will the next one, and the prefix earns at most a note beside the box.

Nine sentences, not one shrug. A refused key, a key not allowed the API, a project with the API off, a key locked to other sites, a spent allowance, a model this account cannot have, a blocked network, a timeout and an outage are nine problems with nine fixes. Where the app can name the fix, it names it.

Nothing you wrote is at risk. The draft is untouched until a reply comes back and passes validation; malformed, empty, wrong-shaped, or carrying a promise about health or money, and it is dropped rather than spoken. Requests give up after thirty seconds, a Stop button stands the whole time, and if you keep typing the newer words win.

07

What changed across iterations

First

A text box and the browser’s speech engine. It worked, and it sounded different on every device — which turned out to be the whole product problem.

Then

Studio voices with an offline cache. Two voices of the app’s own, keyed by a hash of the exact line, so a loop you have heard plays with the network off.

Then

The breathing guide, then the rooms. The visualiser stopped being decoration once every room ran on the guide’s own clock rather than its own.

Then

The player lost its settings column and gained an expanded view and one Adjust sheet — the same control on a phone, on a desktop, and full-screen.

Then

Live sound changes. Crossfading a new ambience under a running loop, from three different places, without restarting anything.

Then

MP3 export from any saved loop, rendered in a Web Worker, with iOS Share alongside Download because a web app has no downloads folder there.

Latest

“Say the app’s name, and stop saying everything else twice.” A pass over duplicated copy, and the writing helper made honest about which engine wrote a suggestion.

08

What comes next

  • Voices that carry the session’s own pacing. Ivy and Fen read a line beautifully and read every line at the same tempo; a guided session wants to slow down as it goes.
  • The rooms, on lower-powered phones. CSS and canvas rather than WebGL was the right call, and there is still a device or two where Stillness is the honest choice and the app should say so.
  • Sharing a loop as audio, not just as a link. The MP3 pipeline already exists; what is missing is making the share sheet the obvious end of it.
09

Where to find it

Open it, type a line, press play. On iOS it adds to the home screen from Safari’s Share menu; elsewhere the browser offers Install, and so does the app.

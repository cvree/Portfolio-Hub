Selected work

Six flagship products,
and what they taught me.

Each case study opens on the most interesting thing the product actually does, then answers the same nine questions: what it is, why I built it, the interaction worth showing, the problem underneath, what it can do today, the decisions behind it, what changed across iterations, what is next, and where to go and use it.

Every screenshot on this site was captured from the running application with real data in it. Where a repository is private, this site says so rather than pointing at a link that will not open.

How status is labelled

Live
Deployed and reachable. Anyone can open it right now.
In development
Runs, is being worked on, and is not publicly deployed today.
Experiment
Built to answer one question. Kept because the answer was useful.
Concept
A repository with a premise and not yet a product. Named, not oversold.

Flagships

The six.

Ordered by how much of my thinking they hold, not by how recently they were pushed.

  1. 01 / Live multiplayer

    SpellBomb

    Real-time party spelling, in a room the server owns.

    Two modes over one set of rooms — a bomb passed clockwise on a fuse, and a sixty-second honeycomb race — plus reconnects, spectators, chests, a leaderboard and a Discord Activity that is the same client and the same rooms as the website.

    In development Node · Socket.IO · Postgres Private repository

    Read the case study →

  2. 02 / Health tooling

    Health Journal

    A private health record that never leaves the phone.

    You design the check-in; it gives you a timeline, sunlight sessions drawn from real solar arithmetic, personal experiments graded on evidence rather than enthusiasm, and lab results that arrive into their own history with your journal drawn underneath them.

    Live React · Vite · PWA No account, no server

    Read the case study →

  3. 03 / Certification study

    Phlebotomy Exam Prep

    Built while training for the exam it prepares you for.

    513 original questions, 181 vocabulary terms, four Order of Draw drill modes, tube-and-additive mastery in four directions, three timed mock papers that prefer questions you have not met, and a readiness score that shows its own working.

    Live Next.js · TypeScript 257 tests

    Read the case study →

  4. 04 / Guided audio

    Manifester

    Words you wrote, read back over a room that breathes.

    A ritual builder and a player. Two studio voices that sound the same on every device, six visualiser rooms driven by the breathing guide, live background-sound changes that crossfade under a running loop, and an MP3 export rendered entirely on the device.

    Live React · Vite · PWA Kokoro-82M voices

    Read the case study →

  5. 05 / Computer vision

    OWCS Comp Tracker

    Every detection sits beside the frame it was read from.

    A $0, no-build pipeline: broadcast VOD in, auditable hero-composition timeline out. Automatic HUD calibration, live-versus-replay frame classification, template-matched detection, a temporal swap model, and a human review gate before anything is called a fact.

    Live Python · OpenCV · SQLite Independent fan project

    Read the case study →

  6. 06 / Research tooling

    PaperAnimator

    The highlight is the citation.

    Open a paper, mark a passage, drop an instrument on it — and a scene appears carrying the exact quads of what you marked. Twenty-one entrances from one definition shared by the canvas, the storyboard and the exporter, so the preview cannot promise what the export will not deliver.

    Live React 19 · TypeScript · WebCodecs Nothing is uploaded

    Read the case study →

Also built

Smaller pieces,
and open experiments.

Named honestly. A repository with a good premise and an early build is listed as exactly that — the flagships above are where the finished thinking lives.

Remy Dee

Greek and Latin root learning: two parts you already know, one medical term you do not. The same premise as the vocabulary half of Phlebotomy Exam Prep, tried first as a game.

Experiment

Medical Terminology Trainer

Drilling the vocabulary that every clinical course assumes you have already absorbed. An early sketch of the spaced-repetition work that later became a real product.

Experiment

PhlebLearn

The first attempt at phlebotomy study material, before the question bank and the drills were the point. Kept because it is where the shape of the real one was worked out.

Experiment

EpiMap

Public-health data on a map — the coursework instinct from epidemiology and biostatistics, pointed at something you can look at rather than tabulate.

Concept

Steady

A small habit-and-consistency tool. Its lesson — that a streak is a worse motivator than a legible record — went straight into Health Journal.

Concept

TwoDO

Two things to do today, and nothing else on screen. An exercise in how much a to-do app can refuse to show you and still be useful.

Experiment

Story Atlas

Mapping a narrative as a place rather than a sequence. The spatial thinking that later made PaperAnimator’s storyboard feel obvious.

Concept

Twelve static pages, four self-hosted typefaces, one stylesheet and one small script. No framework, no CDN in the critical path, and everything readable with JavaScript switched off.

01

What it is

A study platform for phlebotomy students, targeting the NHA Certified Phlebotomy Technician first. Certification is configuration rather than structure, so ASCP PBT and NCCT can be added without a rebuild.

It is also built for California candidates. Beside the national material is a study area for the CDPH Laboratory Field Services CPT1/CPT2 licensing pathway California layers on top — the part hardest to find written down anywhere.

02

Why I built it

I completed the coursework in July 2026 and I am preparing for the NHA CPT exam. Every study resource I found did the same thing: gave me a score, and left the work — what do I do about that score? — to me.

A score is not a study plan. Seventy-two percent does not say whether the misses clustered in one domain, whether they were recall or reasoning, or whether the right answers were luck. So the promise is not “pass your exam” — it is know what to study, which the software can actually keep.

One unexpected side effect: writing 513 questions and 181 definitions that survive their own test suite is the most thorough way to study the material I have found.

03

The most interesting interaction

The Order of Draw drill. This is the knowledge that has to be automatic: get it wrong and additive carries over from the previous tube, and nobody downstream can tell. Every student learns the mnemonic. Almost nobody learns the reason.

So the drill has four modes, and they test four different things:

  • Arrange the sequence — put all six CLSI collection positions in order from scratch.
  • What next — continue a draw already under way, which is the version the chairside actually asks.
  • Spot the error — find the one tube out of place in an otherwise correct sequence.
  • Fill the gaps — complete a part-built sequence.

Every answer names what was misplaced and why each position sits where it does. The side panel calls the mnemonic scaffolding: “Once the sequence is automatic, work on the reason behind it — exam questions are more often about carryover than about reciting colors.” Beside it, the thing nobody tells students until they are on a floor: read the label, not the cap. Cap colours vary by manufacturer.

04

The problem being solved

Exam prep fails students in two ways, and the app is designed against both.

It reports the wrong number. A percentage summarises the past; a student needs a decision about the next hour. So the results screen leads with the weak area, not the score; mastery per area runs Not started to Strong, weighted toward recent answers and gated on exposure; and every recommendation is deterministic and says why in plain language.

It re-serves the same questions. A mock exam you have already sat is a memory test. So every paper prefers questions absent from your last three, and freshness never shortens a paper: once unseen questions run out, seen ones fill it. The bank is deep enough that three consecutive full papers share no question, and a test fails the build if any area gets too shallow for that.

Readiness measures how much of the material you have demonstrated recently, and the screen says what it is not: “It is not a prediction of whether you will pass, and we make no claim that it is.”

05

Current capabilities

  • 513 original certification-style questions across ten study areas, with immediate inline feedback: the verdict, the correct answer, a full explanation, why the specific distractor you picked was wrong, and a memory tip where one helps.
  • Six session modes — Quick 10, all areas, a single area, weak areas, missed questions, and unseen questions. Sessions autosave and resume after a refresh.
  • Order of Draw, four modes over the six CLSI collection positions, with three interchangeable interactions and full keyboard operation.
  • Tube & additive mastery in four directions — tube to additive, additive to tube, tube to specimen type, tube to common use — with the mechanism explained after every answer. Distractors as true as the answer are rejected, so no question has two defensible options.
  • 181 vocabulary terms, each with what it is, what it is part of (EDTA belongs to the lavender tube; the median cubital vein to the antecubital fossa), its word parts, why it matters at the chairside, a mnemonic where one helps, and the terms it is genuinely confused with.
  • Five study modes per set — flashcards, adaptive Learn that promotes recognition to typed recall, Write with near-miss spelling graded “close” rather than wrong, timed Match, and a no-feedback Test — over thirteen sets, with spaced repetition scheduling every term individually.
  • Three timed mock papers with a wall-clock timer that survives a sleeping phone or a closed tab, a question grid with answered and flagged state, submit confirmation, and a domain breakdown afterwards.
  • Long-form study guides on order of draw, tube colours and additives, specimen handling, venipuncture complications and California CPT1/CPT2 licensing — each linking directly into the matching practice or drill.
  • No account. Progress lives in the browser, exports to JSON, and can be reset.
06

Design & technical decisions

Warm ivory, not clinical white. Study happens in long sittings, often at night: a warm off-white surface with a faint grid, a deep medical teal accent, and questions set in a serif you can read across a desk. Tubes are drawn in their real cap colours, because that is the thing being learned.

Three interactions for one action. The arrange drill takes a pointer drag, a two-tap swap or per-row buttons — not because drag is unreliable, but because one-handed in a break room it is the wrong instrument. All three are keyboard-operable, and each row announces its position and what moving it would do.

Content mistakes fail CI. 257 tests cover where a bug would quietly mislead a student: scoring, question selection, mock-paper construction and freshness, mastery and readiness, weak-area detection, recommendation rules, storage migrations, streaks, order-of-draw grading and tube-drill generation — plus the structure of the question bank itself, so a content mistake fails the build rather than reaching a student.

No server, on purpose. Progress is the student’s own business, and an account means a breach risk and a login screen between them and the material. It builds to a static export and hosts anywhere.

Search covers what people type. The glossary search matches the term, its abbreviations and its definition, so “purple tube” finds EDTA.

07

What changed across iterations

First

A question bank and a score. The version that proved the score was not the useful part.

Then

Explanations that name the distractor. The right answer teaches one fact; why the option you chose was wrong teaches the distinction the exam is testing.

Then

Mastery, readiness and deterministic recommendations — the point where results stopped being a report and started being a next step, with the reasoning shown in plain language.

Then

Drills split out from practice. Order of draw and tube mastery are procedural, not propositional; multiple choice was the wrong instrument for both.

Then

Vocabulary became its own product — thirteen sets, five study modes, spaced repetition — once it was obvious most wrong answers were vocabulary failures in a clinical question’s clothes.

Then

California CPT1/CPT2. The national exam is half of what a California candidate navigates, and the state pathway is hard to find written plainly.

Latest

The bank expanded to 513 questions and three mock forms — deep enough that three consecutive full-length papers share no question, with a test that enforces it.

08

What comes next

  • Sit the exam. The most useful next iteration is the one written after I have taken the NHA CPT myself and know which of my assumptions about it were wrong.
  • Sources and caveats on every content item. The app marks its content as awaiting sources and review, on screen rather than hidden. Citing each item to CLSI, NHA and CDPH material is the next real piece of work.
  • A second certification. ASCP PBT is configuration, not architecture — but its blueprint differs enough that the area weightings need research, not a copy.
  • Clinical-scenario questions. The bank is strong on recall and application; the hardest items are short scenarios with a judgement call, and those are the slowest to write well.

Manifester

A tiny calm garden for your intentions.

Write or paste the words you want to hear, choose a voice, and let them loop over gentle ambient sound for as long as you like. It speaks in its own voice — the same one on every device — keeps every line it has ever spoken so a loop plays offline, and installs to a phone’s home screen like a normal app.

Nothing on this site autoplays sound, including this page. Manifester only speaks when you press play.

01

What it is

Two halves. Create is a ritual-building workspace: a title, a large text box that takes anything from one line to a twenty-minute script, a session length, and a live preview that breathes with your chosen pattern, cycles your lines the way the loop will read them, and takes its colour from the ambience you picked. Player is the calmest screen in the app and now the whole of it — a breathing guide, the words being spoken, the controls that matter, and nothing else at all.

Voices
Two studio voices, Ivy and Fen, plus your device’s own
Rooms
Six visualisers, driven by the breathing guide
Sessions
5–30 min, custom up to 8 hours, or “until I stop”
Export
Real MP3, rendered on-device, nothing uploaded
02

Why I built it

I am interested in the unglamorous levers on how a day goes — sleep, sunlight, breathing, what you say to yourself before something hard. The breathing one has the best evidence behind it and the worst software around it: apps that want a subscription before they will say a sentence, that cannot work on a plane, and that read your own words back in a robotic voice that undoes the entire point.

I wanted the version where the words are yours, the voice is good, it works with the network off, and nothing about it is a negotiation. It also happens to be the most demanding audio-engineering problem I have taken on: speech, layered ambience, a breathing clock and a session timer that all have to stay in step while the user changes things underneath them.

03

The most interesting interaction

Changing the background sound without stopping the session. A small control sits in the corner of the stage — including in the expanded view, where it is one of only three things on the screen. It opens one list: silence, every built-in ambience, every sound you have imported, and your playlist if you have one.

Pick one and it crossfades under the running loop. The voice keeps going. The clock keeps going. The breath keeps going. The pass count does not reset. Nothing restarts. The same is true from the Adjust sheet and from the Library while a session runs.

That sounds small and is the hardest thing in the product. It is the difference between an app that plays audio and an app you can be inside — because the moment a session stops to accommodate a preference, the session is over and you are operating software again.

The same rule governs the rest of the player. Voice, speed and pitch change without leaving the screen: the running speech loop is re-optioned rather than restarted, so each change lands on the next line and the loop never breaks step. The delay between loops is counted down on screen — “next loop in 6s” — and frozen exactly where it was if you pause.

04

The problem being solved

A browser’s built-in speech is not the same product on two devices. The same page read by the same code sounds like a different app on a Pixel, an iPhone and a Windows laptop — and on many devices the default voice is a legacy formant synth that makes a calming line sound like a train announcement. A loop you saved has to read the same way wherever you play it, or it is not a thing you own.

So Manifester ships two studio voices of its own — Ivy and Fen, Kokoro-82M, running on this project’s own service, free, with no account, no API key and no metering. And it caches aggressively: a line you have heard once plays instantly forever after, from that device, with no connection at all, because every clip is keyed by a hash of exactly what it is.

The fallback is designed, not accidental. Your device’s own voices are still there for anyone who prefers one, and they are what reads the words if the studio voice cannot be reached — a session never goes quiet because a network did. Device voices are scored by quality and the best one wins automatically, each labelled honestly: Neural, Enhanced, Standard, or Basic — robotic, worth avoiding. When the best available one is poor, the app shows the exact settings path to download a free neural voice for that platform.

One written line is one spoken line. The voice is given a line at a time and reports back the moment it starts speaking one, so the player shows exactly that text. Only a line too long to say in one breath is split further — at sentence, then clause, then word boundaries, in that order — which is also what stops browsers cutting a long passage off part-way through.

05

Current capabilities

  • A breathing guide in eight forms with ten patterns, five breath voices and custom timing for every phase, reporting phase progress on a thin ring with the phase name and a countdown in the centre. It pauses and resumes with the session.
  • Six rooms. The whole screen becomes a space that expands and settles with the guide: Atmosphere, Rings, Waterline, Curtains, Starfield and Stillness — or Drifting, which moves between all six, ninety seconds at a time, crossfading over four. Each is drawn as a circle centred on the orb itself, measured rather than guessed, and nothing with an edge ever crosses it.
  • An expanded view, one tap from the corner of the stage: the player grows into the whole screen and the header and navigation step back until nothing is left but the words, the breath and the controls. It is the same player throughout — the session, the clock, the pass count and the audio carry straight on.
  • Everything else, one tap away, in one place. Adjust opens a single sheet in the order things are wanted, on a phone and on a desktop alike. Choosing a row closes the sheet and opens the one it is about, rather than stacking a second scrim on the first.
  • Download the loop as a real MP3 — five minutes to two hours, containing your voice, repeated with your chosen delay, over your background sound, at your volume balance. Rendered on your device in a Web Worker with a WAV fallback, so the app stays usable while it works and nothing is ever uploaded. Available from any saved loop, not just the one in front of you.
  • Nothing you play is lost. Starting a session writes it to the library on its way to the player, so words you listened to but never got around to saving are waiting under Recent plays. The last twelve are held; nothing you saved is ever pruned.
  • An offline writing helper that reads what you have written, works out what it is reaching for, and appends lines in the same direction — or rewrites what is there into the present tense and the first person, turning an avoided feeling into the thing you actually want. Both are one tap and both are undoable.
  • Optional AI, with your own key. One provider, one guided flow that spells out every step and what it costs. Off by default, switchable off in one control in the two places people look, and it falls back to the offline engine whenever the network or the key gives out.
  • Backend-free sharing, an optional calendar event, and listening totals kept on the device and shown without streaks or goals.
06

Design & technical decisions

Returning is one step. The app restores the most recently played saved loop on launch, so the common path is open → play. Five things stay on the Create screen — title, words, session length, Start and Save — and everything else lives behind Customize your ritual, a list of rows that each state their own value (Moon Garden · 40%, Calm · 4 in / 6 out, 3-second delay) and open in a sheet.

The mixing desk had to go. There used to be a column of settings beside the stage. It made a ritual space look like a control surface, and on a phone it put those controls below a full-height stage, where they were clutter without being reachable. Nothing was removed to fix it — the sliders are the same live ones, the rows open the same sheets — but there is now one control instead of two layouts.

A key is checked by using it, never by looking at it. Connect spends one very short real request, and the word “connected” means that request came back. This matters more than it sounds: Manifester used to insist a Google key began with AIza, and when Google moved AI Studio onto keys beginning AQ., every new key in the world was refused before a request was ever made. Both formats work now, so will the next one, and the prefix is used for at most a friendly note beside the box.

Nine sentences, not one shrug. A refused key, a key not allowed to use the API, a project with the API switched off, a key locked to other sites, a spent daily allowance, a model this account cannot have, a blocked network, a timeout and an outage are nine different problems with nine different fixes. Where the app can name the fix, it names it.

Nothing you wrote is ever at risk. The draft is not touched until a reply has come back and passed validation; a reply that is malformed, empty, the wrong shape, or carrying a promise about health or money is dropped rather than spoken. Requests give up after thirty seconds, there is a Stop button the whole time one is in flight, and if you keep typing while it thinks, the newer words win.

07

What changed across iterations

First

A text box and the browser’s speech engine. It worked, and it sounded different on every device — which turned out to be the whole product problem.

Then

Studio voices with an offline cache. Two voices of the app’s own, keyed by a hash of the exact line, so a loop you have heard plays with the network off.

Then

The breathing guide, then the rooms. The visualiser stopped being decoration once every room ran on the guide’s own clock rather than its own.

Then

The player lost its settings column and gained an expanded view and one Adjust sheet — the same control on a phone, on a desktop, and full-screen.

Then

Live sound changes. Crossfading a new ambience under a running loop, from three different places, without restarting anything.

Then

MP3 export from any saved loop, rendered in a Web Worker, with iOS Share alongside Download because a web app has no downloads folder there.

Latest

“Say the app’s name, and stop saying everything else twice.” A pass over duplicated copy, and the writing helper made honest about which engine wrote a suggestion.

08

What comes next

  • Voices that carry the session’s own pacing. Ivy and Fen read a line beautifully and read every line at the same tempo; a guided session wants to slow down as it goes.
  • The rooms, on lower-powered phones. They are CSS and canvas rather than WebGL, which was the right call, and there is still a device or two where Stillness is the honest choice and the app should say so.
  • Sharing a loop as audio, not just as a link. The MP3 pipeline already exists; what is missing is making the share sheet the obvious end of it.
09

Where to find it

Open it, type a line, press play. On iOS it adds to the home screen from Safari’s Share menu; on Android and desktop the browser offers an Install button, and so does the app.
